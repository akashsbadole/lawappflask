Case Types

Civil cases involve disputes between individuals or organizations, typically involving contracts, property, personal injury, or family law matters.

Criminal cases involve prosecutions by the government against individuals or organizations accused of committing crimes.

Corporate cases involve business-related matters such as mergers and acquisitions, corporate governance, securities, and compliance issues.

Family cases involve legal issues related to family relationships such as divorce, child custody, adoption, and domestic violence.

Employment cases involve workplace disputes such as wrongful termination, discrimination, harassment, and wage and hour violations.
Case Statuses

The case has been opened but work has not yet begun. Initial assessment and planning are in progress.

Active work is being done on the case. This includes research, document preparation, court appearances, negotiations, etc.

Work on the case has been temporarily paused, often awaiting client input, court decisions, or other external factors.

The case has been completed and all necessary final actions have been taken. No further work is planned.
Billing Methods

The client is billed based on the number of hours worked on the case at an agreed-upon hourly rate. Time entries must be tracked accurately.

A fixed fee is charged for the entire case, regardless of the actual time spent. This provides cost certainty for the client.

The attorney's fee is a percentage of the amount recovered for the client. If the case is lost, no fee is charged.

The client pays a fixed amount upfront, which is held in a trust account and billed against as work is performed.
